The Mathematical Breakdown: General relativity treats space and time as a smooth, continuous fabric. Quantum mechanics, however, relies on discrete, fluctuating, and probabilistic events. When you apply the math of quantum fields to gravity, you end up with infinite probabilities (a mathematical dead-end called "non-renormalizability").
The Scale of Observation: Quantum gravitational effects only become apparent at the Planck scale (about
 meters). Probing this scale requires colossal energy levels that far exceed what our most powerful particle accelerators can generate.
Lack of Experimental Data: Because gravity is extraordinarily weak (about
 times weaker than the electromagnetic force), physicists currently have no observable data from the subatomic quantum realm to test competing theories.
The Problem of Time: In standard quantum theory, time is an external, fixed tick on a clock. In general relativity, time is dynamic, flexible, and tied to gravity. Quantizing spacetime causes time to mathematically disappear, resulting in a seemingly static model.
