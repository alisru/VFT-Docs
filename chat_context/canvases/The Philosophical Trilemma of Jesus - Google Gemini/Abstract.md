1. The Epistemological Trilemma and Action as Collateral

The classic trilemma formulated by C. S. Lewis asserts that when an individual claims divine identity and the unilateral authority to forgive moral debt against reality, one cannot relegate them to the category of a "great human moral teacher." The claim admits only three logical statuses:

Deception (Liar): Conscious falsification of reality.

Delusion (Lunatic): Unconscious departure from ontological coherence.

Ontological Veracity (Lord): Accurate identification with the ground of being.

To strip the narrative of its divine claim while praising its moral aesthetic is an informational error. If the speaker is fundamentally detached from reality regarding their own identity, their moral system is rooted in hallucination.

1.2 Action as the Collateral of Truth

Talk carries zero thermodynamic cost. Anyone can utter an unconstrained assertion of ultimate authority. In the absence of verifiable collateral, an assertion of ultimate authority sounds identical to clinical madness:

Claim−Collateral=Delusion

In ancient Near Eastern legal and theological frameworks, forgiving another's sins violated jurisdictional boundaries; only the sovereign ground against whom all infractions occur possesses that prerogative. To claim this authority in sequential history demands proof through consequence.

The crucifixion functions as the ultimate non-reciprocal collateral. By willingly absorbing state-sanctioned execution without violence, retaliation, or recantation, the agent converts a verbal claim into an irreversible physical commitment:

Claim+Terminal Fidelity Under Execution=Proof of Possibility

The narrative does not demand blind faith prior to demonstration; rather, it provides an empirical coordinate where adherence to divine law was tested against terminal mortal pressure and held.
