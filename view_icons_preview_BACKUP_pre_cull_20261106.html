🔲 1. Tiles View Mode: In-Depth Critique & 5 Candidate Designs Needs Selection
Deep Critique: Tiles mode in Windows File Explorer arranges items in multiple horizontal columns. Each tile contains an icon box on the left and TWO text rows on the right (Name above, Type/Size below).
• Why 3.1 failed: Heavy outer card borders at 16×16 eat 4px of horizontal space, turning the inner lines into an illegible solid blob.
• Why 3.2 failed: 4 tiles (2×2) squeezes each tile into 6px width. An icon + text cannot fit inside 6px without becoming pixel noise.
• The Solution: Strip outer container strokes. Ground the icon square on the left, and use distinct line weights/lengths for Title vs Subtitle.
T1: Authentic Minimal Tile Pair Two stacked rows: icon square + title/subtitle
Cleanest At 16px
Pros: Zero clutter, 100% readable at 16px. The bolder top line immediately registers as "File Name" and the shorter muted bottom line as "Details".
T2: Accent-Filled Icon Box Subtle tinted icon square + double text
High Contrast
Pros: The tint on the left square ensures the eye immediately distinguishes the icon from the text, separating it from List view.
T3: Dual Pill Cards (Soft Enclosure) Very delicate outer card outline
Card Aesthetic
Pros: Retains the card boundary without crushing the inside by using 50% opacity on the border and a simplified single title bar.
T4: Two-Column Offset Tiles Shows multi-column wrapping of tiles
Flow Metaphor
Pros: Conveys that Tiles flow sideways into columns. Noticeably distinct from single-column List and Details.
T5: Hero Tile Detail One large tile card showing the full structure
Single Hero
Pros: Focuses on a single magnified tile card. Impossible to blur or clutter at 16px.
