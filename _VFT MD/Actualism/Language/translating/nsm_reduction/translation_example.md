# Translation Examples: Isomorphic Scaling of Primes & Spectrums

This document demonstrates the double-sided nature of the isomorphic scaling system:
1.  **NSM Prime Scaling Examples:** Scaling along distinct semantic axes without collapsing independent anchors.
2.  **Lexicon-Wide Spectral Translation Examples:** Mapping complex, descriptive English words to scaled base-word equivalents.

---

## Part I: NSM Prime Scaling Examples

These examples show how basic NSM sentences are simplified by scaling independent anchors (e.g., substantives, evaluators, descriptors) without collapsing them into each other:

### 1. Scaling Substantives (`I`, `you`, `someone`, `people` preserved as distinct anchors)
*   **Original NSM:** "I see you, and you see many people."
*   **Reduced Isomorphic:** "`I` see `you`, and `you` see many `people`."
*   *Note:* Pronouns and people are distinct, non-collapsed anchors.

### 2. Scaling Evaluators (`good` & `bad` preserved as distinct anchors)
*   **Original NSM:** "I see a good thing, and I see a bad thing."
*   **Reduced Isomorphic:** "I see a `good` `thing`, and I see a `bad` `thing`."
*   *Note:* Pluses are reserved for intensity, e.g., "wonderful" $\rightarrow$ `good+++`, "horrific" $\rightarrow$ `bad+++`.

### 3. Scaling Descriptors (`big` & `small` preserved as distinct anchors)
*   **Original NSM:** "A big animal can do something to a small animal."
*   **Reduced Isomorphic:** "A `big` animal can do `something` to a `small` animal."
*   *Note:* "gigantic" $\rightarrow$ `big+++`, "tiny" $\rightarrow$ `small+++`.

### 4. Scaling Cognition (`know` & `think` preserved as distinct anchors)
*   **Original NSM:** "I know this is true, but someone thinks this is not true."
*   **Reduced Isomorphic:** "I `know` this is true, but `someone` `think` this is not true."
*   *Note:* "proved" $\rightarrow$ `know+++`, "suspected" $\rightarrow$ `think-`.

### 5. Scaling Time Coordinates (`before`, `after`, and `now` preserved as distinct anchors)
*   **Original NSM:** "Before, I did not know this. Now, I know this. After, I will do this for a long time."
*   **Reduced Isomorphic:** "`before`, `I` did not know this. `now`, `I` know this. `after`, `I` will do this for `time+++`."

## Part II: Lexicon-Wide Spectral Translation Examples

These examples show how complex, descriptive English vocabulary is redirected onto base-word axis coordinates using scaling modifier stacks:

### 1. Action, Communication & Collective Attention
*   **Original English:** "The athlete sprinted down the road, shouting at the top of his lungs for everyone to move aside, but the crowd completely ignored him."
*   **Isomorphic Translation:** "The athlete **`run+++`** down the road, **`say+++`** for everyone to move aside, but the crowd completely ignored him."
*   *Breakdown:* "sprinted" $\rightarrow$ **`run+++`** (Max running speed), "shouting" $\rightarrow$ **`say+++`** (Max vocal volume).

### 2. Sensation, Emotion & Quality Evaluation
*   **Original English:** "She adored the magnificent sculpture, but her terrible partner thought it was mediocre."
*   **Isomorphic Translation:** "She **`feel+++`** the **`good+++`** sculpture, but her **`bad+++`** partner **`think`** it was **`good-`**."
*   *Breakdown:* "adored" $\rightarrow$ **`feel+++`** (Max emotional affinity), "magnificent" $\rightarrow$ **`good+++`** (Max quality), "terrible" $\rightarrow$ **`bad+++`** (Max negative quality), "mediocre" $\rightarrow$ **`good-`** (Minimal positive quality).

### 3. Size, Dimension & Space
*   **Original English:** "A tiny insect can easily demolish a gigantic tree if it has enough time."
*   **Isomorphic Translation:** "A **`small+++`** insect can easily demolish a **`big+++`** tree if it has enough time."
*   *Breakdown:* "tiny" $\rightarrow$ **`small+++`** (Minimum size), "gigantic" $\rightarrow$ **`big+++`** (Maximum size).

### 4. Temperature & Time Coordinates
*   **Original English:** "It was freezing cold yesterday, but it will be boiling hot tomorrow."
*   **Isomorphic Translation:** "It was **`cold+++`** yesterday, but it will be **`hot+++`** tomorrow."
*   *Breakdown:* "freezing cold" $\rightarrow$ **`cold+++`** (Max coldness), "boiling hot" $\rightarrow$ **`hot+++`** (Max heat).
