By Peter Brown
In her address to the National Press Club, One Nation leader Pauline Hanson targeted immigration, spending, and social policy, relying on several assertions that clash directly with official data and legislative facts. Below is a breakdown of the key claims and the verified facts.
1. The “51% Migrant” Comparison
The Claim: Hanson questioned whether the electorate supported the “fact” that 51% of people living in Australia were either born overseas or had migrant parents, contrasting this with the United States, which she claimed sat at 14%.
The Fact: While the Australian statistic is accurate (reflecting a highly multicultural first- and second-generation population), her comparison to the US is heavily distorted. According to data from the Pew Research Center, approximately 37% of the US population consists of first- or second-generation migrants – more than double the figure Hanson cited.
2. Late-Term Abortion Claims
The Claim: Hanson claimed that women are terminating pregnancies the day before giving birth, stating: “To abort a baby the day before birth is abhorrent and disgusting, and that’s what I oppose.”
The Fact: Late-term abortions are highly restricted by law across all Australian states and territories. Terminations late in pregnancy require rigorous medical panel or specialist approvals, which are strictly limited to exceptional circumstances – such as severe, fatal fetal abnormalities or significant risks to the mother’s life. Legal and medical experts confirm that a termination “the day before birth” for non-medical reasons simply does not occur
3. Indigenous Affairs Expenditure
The Claim: Hanson questioned accountability regarding a “$30 billion plus a year” spend on Indigenous affairs, implying this entire sum is directed toward race-based, exclusive programs.
The Fact: This figure conflates mainstream government spending with Indigenous-specific funding. Demographic and economic analyses (including historic reviews by the Australian National University) show that the vast majority of expenditure attributed to Indigenous Australians goes toward mainstream public services used by all citizens, such as hospitals, schools, welfare, and policing. Dedicated, Indigenous-specific programs account for only a fraction of that total (under 20%).
4. Language Barriers and Social Cohesion
The Claim: To argue that multiculturalism is failing, Hanson used 2021 Census data showing that 23% of Australians speak a language other than English at home, asking, “How can you generate social cohesion if people can’t speak the language?”
The Fact: Speaking a language other than English at home does not mean a person cannot speak English. The same ABS Census data shows that the vast majority of bilingual or multilingual Australians speak English fluently or very well. Using multi-lingual households as proof of an inability to communicate in English is statistically misleading.
5. Public Broadcaster Axing and Policy Realities
The Claim: Hanson promised that a One Nation government would independently “axe” SBS and reduce the metropolitan ABC to a subscription-only service to save taxpayer funds.
The Fact: The Prime Minister or a ruling party cannot simply dismantle public broadcasters by decree. Both the ABC and SBS are governed by independent federal legislative frameworks (the Australian Broadcasting Corporation Act 1983 and the Special Broadcasting Service Act 1991). Any structural changes, funding removal, or abolition would require passing legislation through both houses of Parliament, presenting a massive constitutional and legislative hurdle.
Verdict: Fail and False.
