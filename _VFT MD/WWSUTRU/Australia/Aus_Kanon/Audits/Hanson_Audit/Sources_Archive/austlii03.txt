R v Hanson; R v Ettridge [2003] QCA 488 (6 November 2003)
SUPREME COURT OF QUEENSLAND

CITATION: R v Hanson; R v Ettridge [2003] QCA 488
PARTIES: R v HANSON, Pauline Lee (appellant/applicant); R v ETTRIDGE, David William (appellant)
JUDGES: de Jersey CJ, McMurdo P and Davies JA
DELIVERED: 6 November 2003, Brisbane
ORDER: Appeal allowed in respect of each appellant; convictions quashed; verdicts of acquittal entered; sentence-leave applications dismissed.

Summary of key findings: Each appellant was convicted at trial (20 August 2003) of dishonestly inducing the Electoral Commissioner to register "Pauline Hanson's One Nation" as a political party, and Hanson additionally of dishonestly obtaining $225,071.07 and $273,566.24 in public funding. The Court of Appeal (de Jersey CJ, McMurdo P, Davies JA) unanimously quashed all convictions and entered acquittals, holding the Crown could not establish beyond reasonable doubt that the 500+ names on the party's membership list were not actually party members (as opposed to members only of the separate "Pauline Hanson Support Movement"). The Court found the evidence (application forms headed "Pauline Hanson's One Nation," processing by party staff, membership cards and receipts issued in the party's name) objectively supported that applicants had joined the political party itself.

Chief Justice de Jersey CJ also noted, obiter, that the jury had not been properly instructed on whether the support movement could itself have counted as a "related political party" under the Electoral Act, which would have independently resolved the issue in the appellants' favour.

McMurdo P's concurring judgment additionally recorded contemporary political reaction to the original conviction and five-week trial, quoting (as reported in the press at the time, not as the Court's own view): the Prime Minister calling the original sentence "a very long, unconditional sentence for what she is alleged to have done"; Bronwyn Bishop likening the prosecution to conduct "under the regime of the tyrant, Robert Mugabe" and stating "we now have a political prisoner"; the NSW Premier calling it "almost a crime without a victim"; and various One Nation figures describing the prosecution as politically motivated. McMurdo P explicitly cautioned that such comments from legislators "could reasonably be seen as an attempt to influence the judicial appellate process."
