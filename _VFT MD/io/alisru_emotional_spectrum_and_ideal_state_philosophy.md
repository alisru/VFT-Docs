# Alisru Emotional Spectrum & Ideal-State Philosophy

## Core Principle

Emotions are not isolated irrational objects.
They are:
- informational trajectory signals
- relative to ideals, anti-ideals, states in time, and perceived movement through possibility-space.

Most emotions contain at least two interacting axes simultaneously:
- movement toward or away from ideal-states
- movement toward or away from anti-ideal states.

This means emotional states are usually:
- vector compositions
rather than:
- single binary conditions.

The framework also treats:
- certainty,
- agency,
- trust,
- trajectory stability,
- relationality,
- state persistence,
- and possibility-space access
as emotionally relevant dimensions.

---

# States in Time

## Definition

States in time are:
- configurations of being,
- identity,
- environment,
- relation,
- or trajectory
considered across temporal persistence.

Goals are desired future states in time.
Anti-goals are avoided future states in time.

Identity itself is treated as:
- probabilistic persistence across state-time
rather than:
- fixed essence.

---

# Ideals and Anti-Ideals

## Ideal

A desired state or trajectory in time.

An ideal can be:
- explicit
- implicit
- personal
- collective
- abstract
- physical
- emotional
- relational
- metaphysical.

Goals are attempts to:
- converge toward ideals.

---

## Anti-Ideal

A state or trajectory considered:
- destructive
- destabilizing
- painful
- restrictive
- existentially threatening
- or undesirable.

Anti-goals are:
- avoidance trajectories relative to anti-ideals.

Example:
- survival goal ↔ death anti-goal.

---

## Motivation

Motivation emerges from:
- perceived distance from ideal
- perceived risk of anti-ideal
- confidence in trajectory efficacy
- emotional valuation of the target state
- trust in the possibility of convergence.

Without an imaginable "better" state:
- constructive motivation weakens.

Pure negation alone is insufficient for stable system-building.

A system can know:
- "this is bad"
without knowing:
- "what stable better looks like."

# Meta-Principle

Most emotions are not singular.
They are:
- layered compositions,
- vector blends,
- or simultaneous multi-axis trajectory evaluations.

Example:
- bittersweetness = grief + gratitude/joy
- nostalgia = valued prior state + irreversible divergence
- anxiety = anti-goal fear + trajectory uncertainty + ideal distrust

Emotions therefore function less like isolated objects and more like:
- dynamic relational state-evaluators.

---

# Emotional Axis Interactions

## Core Dual-Axis Principle

Most emotional states are not generated from only:
- ideal convergence
or:
- anti-ideal avoidance.

Instead, emotions emerge from the interaction between BOTH simultaneously.

Meaning:
- movement toward an ideal can coexist with movement toward an anti-ideal
- movement away from an anti-ideal can occur without movement toward a meaningful ideal
- uncertainty can affect either axis independently
- trust or distrust can apply to:
  - the trajectory,
  - the ideal,
  - the actor,
  - or the environment.

This creates emotionally mixed or layered states.

---

## Ideal Axis

The ideal axis tracks:
- perceived movement toward desired states
- confidence in the desirability of those states
- and confidence in the stability of convergence.

Questions operating on this axis include:
- Is the target desirable?
- Is it reachable?
- Is movement occurring?
- Is convergence stable?
- Would achieving it actually satisfy the need?

Positive movement on this axis tends to produce:
- hope
- excitement
- inspiration
- satisfaction
- meaning
- anticipation
- motivation.

Negative movement tends to produce:
- frustration
- despair
- disillusionment
- emptiness
- depression.

---

## Anti-Ideal Axis

The anti-ideal axis tracks:
- perceived movement toward harmful or destabilizing states
- perceived collision risk
- and confidence regarding avoidance.

Questions operating on this axis include:
- Is danger approaching?
- Is collapse likely?
- Is damage avoidable?
- Is instability increasing?
- Is survivability threatened?

Increasing anti-ideal pressure tends to produce:
- fear
- anxiety
- dread
- panic
- disgust
- anger.

Reduction in anti-ideal pressure tends to produce:
- relief
- peace
- serenity
- safety.

---

## Cross-Axis Emotional Interactions

### Hope + Fear
Can coexist when:
- ideal convergence appears possible
while:
- anti-goal risk remains high.

This often produces:
- tension
- suspense
- emotional volatility.

---

### Anxiety
Not merely fear.

Anxiety often emerges from:
- uncertainty about anti-goal collision
AND/OR:
- distrust regarding the ideal itself.

Examples:
- fear the ideal is unreachable
- fear the ideal is false
- fear achieving the ideal causes hidden anti-goals
- fear trajectory instability.

Anxiety therefore frequently exists as:
- uncertainty between ideal and anti-ideal evaluation.

---

### Depression
Can emerge from multiple axis failures simultaneously:

- lack of meaningful ideal
- inability to perceive reachable future states
- perceived anti-ideal occupancy
- collapse of agency
- prolonged trajectory stagnation.

Depression is often:
- low movement
- low trust
- low possibility-space visibility.

---

### Peace
Not simply pleasure.

Peace often requires BOTH:
- low anti-ideal pressure
AND:
- acceptable ideal-state stability.

This is why temporary pleasure alone does not necessarily produce peace.

---

### Boredom
Occurs when:
- anti-ideal pressure is low
AND:
- meaningful ideal pursuit is absent.

This creates:
- low trajectory stimulation
- low emotional gradient.

---

### Excitement
Occurs when:
- ideal convergence appears imminent
AND:
- trajectory velocity is increasing.

Often paired with:
- uncertainty
- novelty
- or controlled anti-goal risk.

---

### Shock
Occurs when:
- expected trajectory continuity collapses suddenly.

Can result from:
- anti-ideal collision
- impossible ideal collapse
- abrupt state discontinuity
- or sudden reinterpretation of reality.

Shock frequently causes:
- temporary mapping failure.

---

### Courage
Requires BOTH:
- meaningful ideal valuation
AND:
- acknowledged anti-goal risk.

Without anti-goal perception:
- courage reduces to untested movement.

---

### Bittersweetness
Simultaneous:
- grief over divergence from valued state
AND:
- joy/gratitude regarding the existence or preservation of that state.

---

### Nostalgia
Can contain:
- positive valuation of prior state-time
- grief over irreversible divergence
- dissatisfaction with present-state trajectory.

---

### Resentment
Often emerges when:
- ideal pursuit is obstructed
AND:
- anti-ideal pressures appear unfairly distributed.

---

### Envy
Not merely wanting another's state.

Envy often contains:
- perceived ideal possession by another
AND:
- perceived divergence from one's own ideal trajectory.

---

### Jealousy
Frequently contains:
- valuation of shared state continuity
AND:
- fear of anti-goal divergence involving loss.

---

### Love
Can involve:
- desire for shared continuity
- preservation of valued trajectories
- mutual ideal reinforcement
- and anti-ideal protection.

Love therefore often operates on BOTH axes simultaneously:
- preserving ideal convergence
while:
- resisting destabilizing divergence.

---

### Trust
Not blind positivity.

Trust emerges from:
- confidence in cooperative trajectory stability
- confidence in anti-goal avoidance
- confidence in relational consistency across time.

Distrust can emerge from:
- instability
- unpredictability
- contradiction
- hidden incentives
- or repeated divergence.

---

### Hatred
Persistent valuation that:
- a perceived source repeatedly generates anti-ideal outcomes
AND:
- removal/suppression would preserve desired state continuity.

---

### Meaning
Meaning itself may emerge from:
- stable participation in valued trajectories across time.

Loss of meaning often corresponds to:
- collapse of believable ideal trajectories.

---



Emotional intensity can vary according to:
- trajectory velocity
- certainty
- proximity to ideal or anti-ideal
- perceived agency
- relational importance
- persistence duration
- and state-time weighting.

This allows:
- mixed emotions
- dynamic transitions
- and layered emotional states
without forcing binary categorization.

Goals are desired states in time.
Anti-goals are avoided states in time.

Emotions are trajectory reactions relative to those states.
