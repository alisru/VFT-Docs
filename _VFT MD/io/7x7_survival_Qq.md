# 7x7 Survival Matrix — Qq Plane

## 49 Qq Addresses at s \= Micro

## Q1 — WHO: Recognition

Being witnessed as a real person. Not just processed or tolerated — actually seen. When this goes, everything else follows, because the self that would fight to survive has been erased.

**Q1.q1 — Recognition of Identity** Do you feel like you exist as a real, distinct person — even when no one is watching? This is the most basic layer: the sense of being a self that doesn't need someone else to confirm it every minute. *Example:* A person emerging from dissociation and feeling the moment they return to themselves. *Example:* A child absorbed in solo play — identity running fine with no audience required.

**Q1.q2 — Recognition of Potential** Are all of you being acknowledged, or just the parts that are convenient? You can be technically seen while most of who you are is quietly ignored or overwritten. *Example:* A trans person recognised only by their old name and gender — present, but only a sliver of them is being seen. *Example:* A person whose disability gets used to define everything about them, collapsing everything else they are.

**Q1.q3 — Recognition of Position** Does the system actually have to respond to you — or can it just ignore you without consequence? This is about whether your existence is backed up by anything that forces others to take you seriously. *Example:* An NDIS number giving someone the right to funded support — recognition with teeth behind it. *Example:* No fixed address meaning no voter enrolment — position locked behind a door that homelessness closes.

**Q1.q4 — Recognition of Resonance** Does anyone actually care that you exist — not as a case, but as a person? You can have all the paperwork in order and still feel completely invisible to everyone around you. *Example:* A person without legal status who is deeply loved by their community — they matter to people even if the state doesn't see them. *Example:* A person with every right and document in order who still feels like no one would notice if they disappeared.

**Q1.q5 — Recognition of Process** What is the actual process someone goes through to be recognised — and does it work, and who controls it? The mechanism matters as much as the intention behind it. *Example:* A triage system deciding whose need gets seen first — the process is the real gatekeeper. *Example:* An NDIS planning meeting where a person's actual needs get translated into whatever fits the funding categories.

**Q1.q6 — Recognition of Precedent** Why are some people automatically seen while others have to fight just to exist in the room? Something built that gap — and it usually has a long history. *Example:* Colonial classification systems that reduced entire peoples to a single legal label — still shaping who counts today. *Example:* A childhood where being seen felt dangerous or impossible — that history showing up in how someone moves through every room they enter.

**Q1.q7 — Recognition of Consequence** What actually happens to a person — in their body, their mind, their behaviour — when they stop being seen over a long stretch of time? *Example:* Someone getting their NDIS funding back and within months their health, social life, and confidence all start recovering. *Example:* Years of being unseen producing chronic stress responses, isolation, and a body that is aging faster than it should.

## Q2 — WHAT: Purpose

Having a reason to keep going and a future you can actually picture. Not just surviving today but being able to see something worth moving toward.

**Q2.q1 — Purpose of Identity** Are you the one deciding what your life is for — or has that been decided by someone else, or never really decided at all? Purpose needs a self who owns it. *Example:* Someone coming out of a major illness and figuring out what they actually want now — rebuilding themselves as the agent of their own direction. *Example:* A person who has always done what was expected of them and has no real sense of what they would choose.

**Q2.q2 — Purpose of Potential** How wide is your horizon right now — can you see multiple possible futures, or has it narrowed down to just getting through the week? *Example:* Education opening up a range of futures someone's parents never had access to — scope as something that can be genuinely expanded. *Example:* Years of welfare dependency and unstable housing shrinking the view down to the next payment, the next lease, the next crisis.

**Q2.q3 — Purpose of Position** Is what you want to do actually available from where you are — or does geography or access make it practically out of reach? *Example:* A regional town losing its only TAFE — entire futures just removed from the map for everyone there. *Example:* Living close to a city meaning work, education, and community are all within range rather than requiring a move that costs everything.

**Q2.q4 — Purpose of Resonance** Does what you are working toward actually mean something to you — or are you pursuing it because you are supposed to, or because there is nothing else? *Example:* A carer doing work they find genuinely meaningful even though it pays badly and exhausts them — meaning holding the whole thing together. *Example:* Someone who hits all their goals and still feels nothing — all the scope in the world, none of the meaning.

**Q2.q5 — Purpose of Process** Can you actually build a plan and follow it through — or does the gap between wanting something and doing it feel impossible to cross? *Example:* Someone with ADHD or trauma-based executive dysfunction knowing exactly what they want but unable to bridge the gap to action. *Example:* Someone who can break a goal into steps, hold the sequence in mind, and move through it — method working as it should.

**Q2.q6 — Purpose of Precedent** Did the life you were born into open doors or close them — and are those closures still the reason certain futures feel impossible? *Example:* Parents who modelled what having a vocation looks like — a template being passed down as a quiet advantage. *Example:* Schooling disrupted by instability and poverty — the futures that required that foundation just not available now.

**Q2.q7 — Purpose of Consequence** What does it actually do to a person when they lose their sense of direction entirely — and what changes when they find something worth moving toward again? *Example:* Someone who loses their purpose after retirement or a relationship ending and slowly stops functioning across every other area. *Example:* Someone in crisis who finds a new direction and starts recovering across health, relationships, and daily function all at once.

## Q3 — WHERE: Shelter

Having a physical space that keeps you alive and intact — not just a roof, but somewhere your body can actually recover and be safe.

**Q3.q1 — Shelter of Identity** Is your own body maintaining itself — temperature, sleep, basic biology staying stable — or is it having to fight just to keep itself alive? *Example:* The body automatically managing its own temperature, clearing waste during sleep — shelter that runs without you having to think about it. *Example:* Someone sleeping rough in winter whose body is burning everything just trying to stay warm enough to survive the night.

**Q3.q2 — Shelter of Potential** Is where you are actually liveable — or is it technically a roof while the conditions inside are still making you sick? *Example:* Black mould throughout a rental — the building is shelter in name only, it's actively damaging the people inside. *Example:* A basic tent in mild weather — minimal, but enough for the body's actual requirements.

**Q3.q3 — Shelter of Position** The building might be fine while the street outside it is dangerous, or the street is fine while the city has cut off everything you need. Each layer matters separately. *Example:* A solid apartment in a neighbourhood where stepping outside after dark isn't safe — one layer working, another not. *Example:* Safe housing two hours from the nearest GP, support service, or grocery store — shelter present, connection to everything else severed.

**Q3.q4 — Shelter of Resonance** Does where you live feel like home — or does it just feel like somewhere you are staying until something goes wrong again? *Example:* Someone who has moved constantly and never let themselves get attached to any space — technically housed, never home. *Example:* Someone in a run-down place who has put care into it and made it theirs — home despite the state of the building.

**Q3.q5 — Shelter of Process** Are you able to actually keep your space functioning — or is maintaining it costing more energy than you have, especially when the system makes it harder than it should be? *Example:* Executive function difficulties making it genuinely hard to maintain a space — not unwillingness, a neurological cost. *Example:* Someone burning through cognitive energy managing housing paperwork, inspections, and compliance instead of using it on their own recovery.

**Q3.q6 — Shelter of Precedent** How did you end up where you are living — and is the history of how you got here still limiting what you can do about it? *Example:* Housing instability that goes back two or three generations — not a personal failure, a structural inheritance. *Example:* Someone who was stably housed until one health event took their income — the fragility sitting beneath what looked like security.

**Q3.q7 — Shelter of Consequence** What is your living situation actually doing to your health, your sleep, your capacity to think and handle everything else? *Example:* Poor sleep from unsafe or uncomfortable shelter quietly degrading immunity, mood, and cognitive function over months. *Example:* Stable, secure shelter making everything else more manageable — the platform the rest of the matrix needs to stand on.

## Q4 — WHY: Refreshment

Being able to actually recover — water, air, sleep, rest — the inputs that let your body and mind clear out and come back.

**Q4.q1 — Refreshment of Identity** Do you know what actually restores you — and are you allowed to get it? Different people genuinely need different things, and a lot of people never get to find out what theirs is. *Example:* Someone with sensory sensitivities for whom most rest environments are still overstimulating — their restoration needs something specific. *Example:* A carer whose own need for restoration is treated as irrelevant or indulgent — the depleted person invisible inside their role.

**Q4.q2 — Refreshment of Potential** What does your system actually need to recover — is it sleep, quiet, water, beauty, movement, company, time alone — and is that what you are getting? *Example:* Someone who needs solitude to recover and lives in a crowded, noisy environment — the wrong input, no recovery possible. *Example:* Someone realising that making things restores them far more than watching screens — finding the actual content of their own restoration.

**Q4.q3 — Refreshment of Position** Is there actually somewhere you can rest — safely, quietly, without being interrupted or threatened? The need for rest without a place to do it is just more depletion. *Example:* Unsafe or noisy accommodation making deep sleep impossible — rest needed, location making it unavailable. *Example:* A green space nearby giving someone somewhere to decompress daily — a location-level survival resource hiding in plain sight.

**Q4.q4 — Refreshment of Resonance** When you rest, do you come back as yourself — or just as a body that can function again for a while? There is a difference between physical recovery and something deeper. *Example:* Someone who takes all the right breaks and still returns to work feeling exactly as hollow as they left. *Example:* Someone who gets into nature or makes something or prays and comes back genuinely different — Refreshment of Resonance working at a different level.

**Q4.q5 — Refreshment of Process** Do you understand how recovery actually works in your body — or are you doing things that feel like rest but do not actually give your nervous system what it needs? *Example:* Someone using alcohol to wind down, not knowing it breaks sleep architecture and leaves them more depleted the next day. *Example:* Someone who has figured out their own recovery rhythm and protects it — actively managing their own method.

**Q4.q6 — Refreshment of Precedent** How depleted are you going into rest — and is that depletion from something recent or something that has been building for years without being addressed? *Example:* Someone carrying decades of trauma and chronic stress who needs far more recovery than a normal night's sleep — the baseline load is just that high. *Example:* Someone who became depleted from a specific, recent period and has a realistic pathway back to baseline.

**Q4.q7 — Refreshment of Consequence** What happens to everything else — your decisions, your relationships, your health — when you cannot recover? And what opens up when you actually can? *Example:* Sleep deprivation making someone functionally as impaired as if they were drunk — Refreshment failure flowing straight into every other plane. *Example:* Someone who finally gets adequate rest and notices their thinking, patience, and physical health all improve — what was always possible becoming visible.

## Q5 — HOW: Power

Having real agency — the ability to make decisions, take action, and have that action actually change something.

**Q5.q1 — Power of Identity** Are you actually the one running your own life — or is someone or something else making the calls while you go through the motions? *Example:* Someone whose guardian makes every decision about housing, money, and care — their life managed from outside. *Example:* Someone in a controlling relationship where every choice is effectively made for them through pressure, threat, or punishment.

**Q5.q2 — Power of Potential** How many real choices do you actually have right now — not in theory, but in practice, given where you are and what you have? *Example:* Being given a choice between two options that are both inadequate — the form of choice with none of the substance. *Example:* Having legal support in a dispute suddenly making options visible that were invisible before — scope is not fixed, it expands with access.

**Q5.q3 — Power of Position** Does where you are give you things to work with — or does your location cut you off from the levers you need? *Example:* Being geographically isolated from services, transport, and opportunity — every plane constrained by a single location failure. *Example:* Dense urban infrastructure meaning options are available without requiring major logistics — location as a quiet structural advantage.

**Q5.q4 — Power of Resonance** Does taking action feel like it matters anymore — or have enough attempts ended in nothing that it has stopped feeling worth trying? *Example:* Someone who tried for years and was blocked every time slowly stopping — not giving up but being trained by experience to stop. *Example:* Someone whose purpose is strong enough that they keep going despite hostile conditions — meaning as the fuel that keeps agency running.

**Q5.q5 — Power of Process** Can you step back, notice what is not working, and try something different — or do you keep hitting the same wall the same way without being able to adjust? *Example:* Someone who can review their own approach and adapt it — agency that improves itself over time. *Example:* Someone whose nervous system is stuck in a trauma response that overrides deliberate thought — unable to access the meta layer even when they can see what they need to do.

**Q5.q6 — Power of Precedent** Did you grow up in an environment where your actions had real effects on things around you — or were you taught by experience that trying does not work? *Example:* A childhood with consistent, fair consequences and room to experiment — agency growing naturally from experience. *Example:* A childhood where agency was punished, mocked, or simply produced no results — learned helplessness baked in before adulthood.

**Q5.q7 — Power of Consequence** Is using your agency building you over time — or is it being ground down until the capacity itself starts to erode? *Example:* Consistent, effective action building competence, confidence, and expanding what is possible — agency compounding. *Example:* Agency blocked at every turn until the person starts losing the neurological capacity to make decisions at all — not a metaphor, a clinical outcome.

## Q6 — CAUSE: Moral

Living with a conscience that is intact — being able to account honestly for what you have done, carry that without it destroying you, and stay in alignment with your own values.

**Q6.q1 — Moral of Identity** Do you see yourself as someone who is responsible for what you do — or has that sense been stripped away, dissolved by a system, or never really formed? *Example:* Someone who owns a mistake they made and tries to repair it — moral identity working. *Example:* A bureaucracy spreading responsibility so thin across roles and processes that no one person feels accountable for anything — moral identity structurally prevented.

**Q6.q2 — Moral of Potential** Have you actually looked at what you have done and not done — or is that ledger sitting there unread, costing you something every day just to keep it closed? *Example:* Someone who has never examined their own impact — the record is there, they just have not looked at it. *Example:* Someone in therapy going through the specific things they did and did not do — the record coming into view, finally able to be worked with.

**Q6.q3 — Moral of Position** Where did the harm happen, and can you get back to it to repair it — or is the site of the wound somewhere you can no longer reach? *Example:* Someone who hurt people in a relationship or community they have since left and cannot re-enter — the repair pathway physically blocked. *Example:* Someone whose wound and repair both live in their family of origin — hard, but accessible if they choose it.

**Q6.q4 — Moral of Resonance** Is the guilt or discomfort you carry pointing you somewhere useful — or has it just become weight with no direction? *Example:* Someone using their conscience as a compass, letting discomfort tell them where to look and what to change. *Example:* Someone who has carried guilt for decades without it ever moving — not informing action, just accumulating.

**Q6.q5 — Moral of Process** Do you have a way of honestly checking yourself — or is honest self-examination something you avoid because it feels too threatening? *Example:* Someone with a regular practice of reflection, however informal — the process keeping the plane clear. *Example:* Someone for whom looking honestly at themselves triggers shame spirals so severe that avoidance is the only way to function — the process inaccessible.

**Q6.q6 — Moral of Precedent** Where did your sense of right and wrong actually come from — and was it built by something true, or was it built by what you needed to believe to survive? *Example:* Values formed through experience, honest relationships, and exposure to consequences — built on solid ground. *Example:* Values formed in an environment where moral compromise was the price of safety — the foundation warped from the start.

**Q6.q7 — Moral of Consequence** What does living out of alignment with your own conscience actually cost you — not in theory, but in your body, your relationships, and your ability to function? *Example:* The well-documented physical and psychological deterioration of veterans carrying moral injury — not metaphysical, measurable. *Example:* The measurable wellbeing improvements that follow restorative justice — the cost of unresolved moral damage being a real variable, not just a feeling.

## Q7 — EFFECT: Sustenance

Being actually fed — food, yes, but also warmth, safety, and emotional nourishment. The inputs that keep the nervous system from running on fumes.

**Q7.q1 — Sustenance of Identity** Can you feel when you are hungry, depleted, or full — or has that signal gone quiet from years of overriding it or never being allowed to respond to it? *Example:* Someone whose history with food means they genuinely cannot tell when they are hungry — the signal is there but the line is down. *Example:* Someone in acute shock or grief whose body stops sending hunger signals entirely — a temporary disconnection with serious physical consequences.

**Q7.q2 — Sustenance of Potential** Are you actually being nourished in all the ways you need — food, yes, but also safety, warmth, human contact — or just in the most visible one? *Example:* Someone eating enough calories while being in chronic fear — fed by one measure, starving by another. *Example:* Someone who eats alone every day versus someone eating the same food with people they trust — the same input, different outcomes.

**Q7.q3 — Sustenance of Position** Can you actually get food — and is where you eat somewhere that lets it do what it needs to do? *Example:* A neighbourhood with no accessible fresh food and public transport gaps — sustenance gated behind logistics most people never have to think about. *Example:* A table with people you trust and food that was made with care — location adding a layer that compounds the input.

**Q7.q4 — Sustenance of Resonance** Does eating mean something to you beyond keeping the body running — or has it become either purely mechanical or something charged and difficult? *Example:* Someone after a loss who stops cooking, stops eating with others, starts skipping meals — the meaning gone, the body following. *Example:* A food tradition shared across generations — every meal also carrying identity, history, and belonging.

**Q7.q5 — Sustenance of Process** Is your body actually absorbing and using what you eat — or are you feeding yourself and still running on empty because something is blocking the process? *Example:* A digestive condition meaning nutrients are not being absorbed regardless of what or how much is eaten — the method broken independent of access. *Example:* Chronic stress keeping cortisol high enough to suppress digestion — the body unable to use what it is being given because another plane is in crisis.

**Q7.q6 — Sustenance of Precedent** What is the history behind your relationship with food and nourishment — and is that history still shaping how your body and nervous system respond right now? *Example:* Growing up food insecure producing a nervous system in adulthood that still responds to scarcity as if it is constant — the origin running in the background. *Example:* Someone whose relationship with food was stable until illness or injury changed their metabolic needs — origin as the disruption point rather than the chronic condition.

**Q7.q7 — Sustenance of Consequence** What does being consistently well-nourished versus chronically underfed actually do to everything else — your thinking, your moods, your ability to hold everything else together? *Example:* Blood sugar instability producing irritability, poor decisions, and emotional fragility — sustenance failure showing up in the Moral and Power planes before anyone notices the physical cause. *Example:* Someone with stable, adequate nourishment handling stress better, thinking more clearly, and recovering faster from everything else that goes wrong.
