# 7×7×7 Trust Phase-Field Model (Structured Documentation)

## 1. Overview
This document formalises a multi-layer interpretive framework where "trust" is treated as a dynamic, multi-axis coherence field rather than a scalar value. Interaction dynamics emerge from recursive alignment across seven interrogative planes, each decomposed into sublayers (q and c levels), forming a 7×7×7 tensor-like structure.

Trust is modelled as a phase-dependent coupling system that transitions between:
- Repulsive regimes
- Weak coupling regimes
- Strong coupling regimes
- Bulk cohesion (emergent collective agent behaviour)

---

## 2. Core Structure: 7 Interrogative Planes (Q Layer)
Each plane represents a fundamental axis of interpretation and system interaction.

### Q1 – WHO (Metaphysical / Will)
- Identity and intent
- Agent type recognition
- Existential alignment

### Q2 – WHAT (Possible / Probability)
- Future action space
- Hypothetical behaviours
- Distribution of possibility

### Q3 – WHERE (Physical / Spatial)
- Proximity and access
- Structural constraints
- Physical enforceability

### Q4 – WHY (Lyrical / Meaning)
- Narrative coherence
- Semantic resonance
- Shared meaning structures

### Q5 – HOW (Logical / Process)
- Procedural consistency
- Predictability of mechanism
- Internal behavioural logic

### Q6 – CAUSE (Historical / Sequence)
- Memory and track record
- Temporal consistency
- Pattern repetition over time

### Q7 – EFFECT (Emotive / Consequence)
- Outcome impact
- Emotional residue
- Systemic consequence memory

---

## 3. Recursive Structure (q and c Layers)
Each Q-plane decomposes into sublayers:

- q-layer: interrogative refinement of Q (structural subdomain)
- c-layer: contextual or micro-event resolution layer

Example:
- Q2 → q2 → c2
- Each layer refines resolution of possibility, constraint, or interaction depth

This creates a 7×7×7 recursive tensor structure.

---

## 4. Trust as a Multi-Axis Field
Trust is defined as a vector field across planes:

T = {T_Q1, T_Q2, T_Q3, T_Q4, T_Q5, T_Q6, T_Q7}

Each component represents coherence within that axis.

Total trust is not scalar but emergent from:
- cross-plane alignment
- variance reduction across q and c layers
- stability over time

---

## 5. Interaction Regimes (Phase States)
System behaviour emerges from aggregated trust coherence:

### 5.1 Repulsive Strong
- Multi-plane negative alignment
- High friction interaction
- Active avoidance or containment behaviour

### 5.2 Repulsive Weak
- Low interaction frequency
- Neutral or distant relation
- Minimal coupling

### 5.3 Attractive Weak
- Transactional cooperation
- Partial alignment in selected planes

### 5.4 Attractive Strong
- High coherence across most planes
- Stable cooperation and predictive reliability

### 5.5 Bulk Cohesion
- Emergent collective identity formation
- Low-variance alignment across all planes
- System behaves as a unified agent

---

## 6. Event Dynamics and Propagation
Events are modelled as perturbations in one or more Q-planes.

Effects:
- Non-uniform propagation across planes
- Amplification or damping depending on cross-plane resonance
- Recursive feedback into trust structure

An event does not produce a single outcome but a distribution of state shifts across the tensor.

---

## 7. Signal vs Noise Interpretation
System stability depends on:

- Signal: coherent cross-plane alignment
- Noise: divergence, contradiction, or high variance across planes

High noise reduces predictive reliability and destabilises trust coherence fields.

---

## 8. Phase Transitions
Transitions between interaction regimes occur when:

- cross-plane coherence thresholds are crossed
- recursive feedback amplifies or dampens alignment
- system-wide variance shifts beyond stability bounds

These transitions are analogous to phase changes in physical systems but operate on informational and relational structure.

---

## 9. Bulk Cohesion Condition
Bulk cohesion emerges when:

- all 7 planes exhibit stable positive alignment
- q and c sublayers stabilise (low variance)
- interaction feedback loops reinforce coherence

Result:
- emergence of higher-order collective behaviour
- system acts as a unified agent rather than discrete interactions

## 10. Summary
This framework models trust and interaction as a recursive, multi-layer tensor system rather than scalar probabilities. It combines:
- interrogative semantic axes
- recursive substructure (q and c layers)
- phase-transition interaction regimes
- event-driven state perturbation dynamics

The result is a structured model of social, informational, and relational systems as evolving phase-fields over a 7×7×7 interpretive space.

## 11. Dynamic Regime Evolution and Context Saturation
Trust is not static within a regime; it undergoes continuous transformation across interaction density and context exposure.

A key dynamic is the transition pathway:
- weak attractive coupling → moderate coupling → strong short-range coupling → stabilised local coherence field

As interaction frequency and consistency increase within a bounded context space, trust can *condense* into a high-density alignment state.

### Context Saturation Effect
When a trust configuration saturates a local contextual space (i.e. most available interaction pathways within that space align coherently across multiple Q-planes), the system transitions into a state of high observability and structural inevitability.

In this state:
- trust is no longer just relational, but field-like
- it becomes externally detectable as a stable pattern of interaction outcomes
- it begins to act as a generative source of secondary influence propagation

### Recursive Spread of Influence
Once saturated, the trust field can induce downstream coupling effects:
- nearby agents or nodes experience induced alignment pressure
- local probability distributions shift toward the established coherence field
- new interaction regimes form as a function of proximity to the saturated field

This creates a recursive amplification loop:

saturation → observability → influence propagation → secondary alignment → further saturation

This mechanism explains how localized trust structures can scale into bulk cohesion across wider system regions.
