# Community Welfare Survey Instrument (Quad-Vector  $7 \times 7$  Matrix Edition)

## Based on the  $7 \times 4$  Survival Matrix Framework

## 1. Executive Overview & Methodology

The  $7 \times 4$  **Survival Matrix Assessment Tool** evaluates total community viability ( $V$ ) across **Seven Core Survival Requirements**:

1. **Recognition** (*Metaphysical Plane,*  $c_1$ ): Identity, visibility, dignity, and skill utilization.
2. **Purpose** (*Possible Plane,*  $c_2$ ): Trajectory space, future options, mobility, and career pathways.
3. **Shelter** (*Physical Plane,*  $c_3$ ): Physical safety, spatial containment, thermoregulation, and infrastructure.
4. **Refreshment** (*Lyrical Plane,*  $c_4$ ): Restorative rest, aesthetic joy, play, and spiritual renewal.
5. **Power** (*Logical Plane,*  $c_5$ ): Executive agency, governance, democratic access, and decision-making authority.
6. **Morale** (*Historical Plane,*  $c_6$ ): Internal spirit, conviction, historical truth, fortitude, and resilience.
7. **Sustenance** (*Emotive Plane,*  $c_7$ ): Nervous system safety, emotional climate, mutual care, and social cohesion.

### Survey Architecture: The Quad-Vector  $(A, B, C, D)$  Format

Every item in this instrument evaluates a **two-dimensional vector** pairing **Sense (Capacity / Potential)** with **Use (Execution / Realization)**. Each sub-indicator additionally closes with an **optional flag pair**, **Offer (Contributable Capacity)** and **Need (Requested Support)**, asked once per sub-indicator rather than once per scale:

$$\text{Item}_{p, i} = \Big[ \text{\textbf{A (Sense):} Availability, perception, or capacity} \Big] \quad \mathbf{AND} \quad \Big[ \text{\textbf{B (Use):} Active deployment, execution, or impact} \Big]$$

Evaluating both dimensions reveals critical structural failure modes:

* **High** $A$ **, Low** $B$ **(Suppression / Latent Bottleneck):** High latent capacity, skills, or awareness that are suppressed, ignored, or blocked from active realization.
* **Low** $A$ **, High** $B$ **(Coercive Duty / Empty Action):** Action forced upon an individual without underlying agency, security, or self-sense.
* **High** $A$ **, High** $B$ **(Coherent Flourishing):** Full alignment between potential and real-world execution.

### The Flag Pair  $(C, D)$ : From Assessment to Matching

The  $A$  and  $B$  vectors are diagnostic. They describe a respondent's situation and are used to compute plane scores. The  $C$  and  $D$  vectors are **operational flags**: they are optional, they do not enter  $\Phi_p$  or  $V$ , and their sole purpose is to tell a human reviewer what to actually do about a given respondent.

Both flags are themselves **AND-paired**, on the same force-and-resistance logic as  $A$  and  $B$ . The  $a$  term is the force, the  $b$  term is what opposes it:

$$\text{Offer}_{p, i} = \Big[ \text{\textbf{Ca:} Capacity to supply this to another} \Big] \ \mathbf{AND} \ \Big[ \text{\textbf{Cb:} What blocks me giving it} \Big]$$
$$\text{Need}_{p, i} = \Big[ \text{\textbf{Da:} Urgency of my need for this} \Big] \ \mathbf{AND} \ \Big[ \text{\textbf{Db:} What blocks me receiving it} \Big]$$

Note that  $Da$  is **urgency, not lack**. Lack is already measured by a low  $B$  across three scales; a second lack measure would carry no new information. What  $D$  contributes that  $B$  cannot is the respondent's own declaration that they want something done. Deficit is measured, need is declared.

Each blocker line carries a **type tag** alongside its magnitude. Magnitude alone cannot drive an intervention: knowing a respondent is heavily blocked is useless without knowing by what. The give-side and receive-side tag lists differ, because the things that stop a person contributing are not the things that stop them asking.

Together they convert the instrument from a needs assessment into a **matching engine**. A high  $D$  from one respondent and a high  $C$  from another at the *same sub-indicator address*  $(p, i)$  constitute a candidate pairing. The community's own latent supply is measured in the same pass as its demand.

**Why  $D$  is not redundant with a low  $B$ .** A low  $B$  records a deficit; it does not record whether the respondent wants anything done about it. Two people can score identically at 4.4 Aesthetic Joy while one is content and the other is starving for it. Section 2c resolves this at plane level across seven requirements;  $D$  resolves it at sub-indicator level across forty-nine, and does so in the respondent's own voice rather than by inference. **Deficit is measured. Need is declared.** Acting on the first without the second is how outreach becomes intrusion.

Reading the flags:

* **High** $Ca$ **, Low** $Cb$ **(Free Supply):** Willing and unobstructed. Enter directly into the Supply Pool.
* **High** $Ca$ **, High** $Cb$ **(Gated Supply):** The capacity exists and is being held shut by something. The  $Cb$  type tag names what. Removing that one obstacle returns a contributor to the community at far lower cost than sourcing the same capacity externally.
* **High** $Ca$ **, Low** $B$ **(Unused Reserve):** A person capable of supplying a requirement to others while being denied it themselves. The classic signature of an underemployed, isolated, or overlooked community asset.
* **High** $Da$ **, Low** $B$ **(Confirmed Live Need):** Deficit and declared need agree. Highest-confidence referral target.
* **Low** $Da$ **, Low** $B$ **(Tolerated Deficit):** A real deficit the respondent is not asking for help with. Record it in aggregate; do not action it individually.
* **High** $Da$ **, High** $B$ **(Fragile Sufficiency):** Currently adequate but the respondent does not trust it to hold. Often a precursor to acute failure and worth watching.
* **High** $Da$ **, High** $Db$ **(Obstructed Need):** Help is wanted and something is in the way. The  $Db$  type tag distinguishes a missing service from an unreachable one from an unaskable one, which require entirely different responses.
* **High** $Ca$ **, High** $Da$ **(Conditional Contributor):** Both willing to give and needing support. Meeting this person's need frequently unlocks supply for several others, making them disproportionately high-leverage.

### The Unlock Chain

Recording blockers on both sides closes a loop the earlier draft left open. A give-side blocker is very often an unmet need sitting at a different address: a respondent may hold high  $Ca$  at 2.2 Applied Mastery, blocked, with the  $Cb$  tag reading *transport*, which resolves to a  $Da$  at 3.5 Utility Infrastructure. The obstruction is not vague, it is a specific edge between two coordinates in the matrix.

This makes contribution capacity **traceable rather than inferred**, and it means an intervention can be selected by how much supply it releases rather than by how loudly a need was voiced. It also delivers the Module 8.6 Barrier-to-Asking diagnostic at sub-indicator resolution across all forty-nine addresses, instead of once for the whole survey.

### The Time Anchor  $(\text{last})$

Each rating line carries an optional `last [ ]` field. The rating itself is deliberately **untimed**: it captures the respondent's general, immediately-perceived sense of their situation rather than a boxed observation window. Timeboxing the question ("in the past two weeks") would force a reporting frame that most respondents do not naturally hold, and would suppress chronic conditions that are true in general but not acute this fortnight.

The `last` field instead captures **recency of change**, recorded only when the respondent wants to mark it. Entries may be approximate ("2 yrs ago", "since the mill closed", "always"). Its analytic purpose is trajectory: a low score marked *recently changed* is an acute event needing rapid response, while the same score marked *always* or *years* is a chronic structural condition needing a different intervention entirely. The score alone cannot distinguish these.

### Scale & Rating Standard

Each statement vector is rated on a **1 to 5 scale**:

$$\mathbf{1} = \text{Non-Existent / Severe Deprivation } (0.00) \quad \mathbf{2} = \text{Unstable / Marginal } (0.25) \quad \mathbf{3} = \text{Adequate Baseline } (0.50)$$

$$\mathbf{4} = \text{Secure / Strong } (0.75) \quad \mathbf{5} = \text{Flourishing / Fully Realized } (1.00)$$

For the **Offer  $(C)$ ** vector the same 1-5 scale is read as capacity to contribute:

$$\mathbf{1} = \text{Not at all } \quad \mathbf{2} = \text{Rarely or with difficulty} \quad \mathbf{3} = \text{Occasionally} \quad \mathbf{4} = \text{Readily} \quad \mathbf{5} = \text{Freely and often}$$

For **Need urgency  $(Da)$ ** it is read as strength of requested support:

$$\mathbf{1} = \text{Not needed } \quad \mathbf{2} = \text{Would be nice} \quad \mathbf{3} = \text{Would genuinely help} \quad \mathbf{4} = \text{Needed} \quad \mathbf{5} = \text{Urgent}$$

For both **blocker terms  $(Cb, Db)$ ** it is read as strength of obstruction:

$$\mathbf{1} = \text{Nothing stops me } \quad \mathbf{2} = \text{Minor friction} \quad \mathbf{3} = \text{Real obstacle} \quad \mathbf{4} = \text{Mostly blocked} \quad \mathbf{5} = \text{Completely blocked}$$

All  $C$  and  $D$  terms are **optional and unscored**. Leaving them blank carries no penalty and does not affect any plane score. They exist to flag a respondent to a human reviewer, not to grade them.

Respondents may skip any item. Skipped items are **not** treated as a middling score; see Section 3, Step 7.

## 3. Vector Computation & Scoring Methodology

### Step 1: Normalize Raw Items (1–5 Scale to 0.00–1.00)

Convert each rated item ( $X \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ ) into a normalized score ( $x \in [0.00, 1.00]$ ):

$$x = \frac{X - 1}{4}$$

### Step 2: Compute Vector Scores for Each Sub-Indicator

Each sub-indicator yields two normalized vector scores across Micro, Meso, and Macro scales:

$$\text{Sense Score } (A_{p, i}) = \frac{A_{\text{Micro}} + A_{\text{Meso}} + A_{\text{Macro}}}{3}$$

$$\text{Use Score } (B_{p, i}) = \frac{B_{\text{Micro}} + B_{\text{Meso}} + B_{\text{Macro}}}{3}$$

$$\text{Realization Efficiency Index } (\eta_{p, i}) = \frac{B_{p, i}}{A_{p, i} + \epsilon}$$

*(Where* $\eta < 0.70$ *indicates systemic suppression / blocked potential).*

### Step 3: Compute Requirement Plane Score ( $\Phi_p$ )

Calculate the combined plane viability score ( $\Phi_p$ ) using the geometric mean of total Sense and total Use across all 7 sub-indicators:

$$\Phi_p = \sqrt{\left( \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{7} A_{p, i}}{7} \right) \cdot \left( \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{7} B_{p, i}}{7} \right)}$$

### Step 4: Total Systemic Viability Index ( $V$ )

$$V = \prod_{p=1}^{7} \Phi_p$$

*Note: the product form is deliberate and non-compensatory. A near-zero score on any single plane collapses  $V$  toward zero regardless of performance elsewhere, on the principle that a surplus of Recognition does not substitute for an absence of Shelter. This is a weakest-link model, not an averaging model.*

### Step 5: Scale Divergence Index ( $\Delta_{p, i}$ )

The Micro, Meso, and Macro responses are averaged into  $A_{p,i}$  and  $B_{p,i}$  above, but the *disagreement between scales* is itself diagnostic and must be retained separately:

$$\Delta_{p, i} = B_{\text{Macro}} - B_{\text{Micro}}$$

Interpretation:

| $\Delta$ | Pattern | Reading | Intervention Class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\Delta \ll 0$ | High Micro, Low Macro | The person is coping, the system is not. Individual resilience is compensating for institutional absence. | **Institutional failure.** Build or repair the service. The individual needs no fixing. |
| $\Delta \gg 0$ | Low Micro, High Macro | The provision exists but is not reaching this person. | **Access failure.** Referral, navigation support, outreach, or removal of an eligibility barrier. |
| $\Delta \approx 0$ | Aligned | Individual and system agree. | Score is trustworthy at face value. |

A community-wide pattern of  $\Delta \ll 0$  in a plane indicates institutional collapse being privately absorbed. A pattern of  $\Delta \gg 0$  indicates services that exist on paper and fail in delivery. These require opposite responses and are indistinguishable from the plane score alone.

### Step 6: Offer Surplus and the Matching Ledger

For each sub-indicator address  $(p, i)$ , compute across all respondents:

$$\text{Demand Pool}_{p,i} = \{ r : Da_{r,p,i} \geq 0.75 \} \qquad \text{Supply Pool}_{p,i} = \{ r : Ca_{r,p,i} \geq 0.75 \ \wedge \ Cb_{r,p,i} \leq 0.50 \}$$

$$\text{Gated Supply}_{p,i} = \{ r : Ca_{r,p,i} \geq 0.75 \ \wedge \ Cb_{r,p,i} > 0.50 \}$$

The Supply Pool holds contributors who can act now. Gated Supply holds contributors who cannot, and is tracked separately rather than discarded, because it is recoverable capacity.

$$\text{Match Ratio } (\mu_{p,i}) = \frac{|\text{Supply Pool}_{p,i}|}{|\text{Demand Pool}_{p,i}| + \epsilon}$$

* $\mu \geq 1$ : the community **already contains** the capacity to meet this need. The shortfall is a connection problem, not a resource problem. Act by introducing people, subject to consent.
* $\mu < 1$ : genuine shortfall. External resource, funding, or service provision is required.

This distinction is the operational core of the instrument. It separates needs the community can meet internally from needs that require outside intervention, and it does so from a single pass of data collection.

**Demand is built from  $D$ , not from  $B$ .** Only declared need enters the matching ledger. A low  $B$  with no accompanying  $D$  is counted in the aggregate statistics and in  $\Phi_p$ , but is never entered into a match or a referral list. This is a consent boundary, not a data-quality one: a person's deficit is the instrument's business, but acting on it is theirs to authorise.

**Unmet Declaration Rate  $(\delta_{p,i})$ .** Track separately the respondents with low  $B$  and low or blank  $D$ :

$$\delta_{p,i} = \frac{|\{ r : B_{r,p,i} \leq 0.25 \ \wedge \ Da_{r,p,i} < 0.75 \}|}{|\{ r : B_{r,p,i} \leq 0.25 \}|}$$

A high  $\delta$  means a community in real deficit that is not asking for help. Read alongside Module 8.6 (Barrier to Asking), it distinguishes contentment from resignation, stigma, or not knowing that help exists. Resignation is not consent, and a plane with high deficit and high  $\delta$  warrants a different response entirely: not matching, but making it safe and legible to ask.

**Demand is built from  $Da$ , not from  $B$ .** Reiterated because it is the consent boundary of the whole instrument.

### Step 6b: Unlock Ledger

For every respondent in Gated Supply, the  $Cb$  type tag names the obstruction. Resolve each tag to the sub-indicator address that supplies it (*transport* to 3.5, *care duties* to 7.2, *no time* to 4.2, *money* to 2.3 or 2.5, *skills/confidence* to 2.4, *permission/legal* to 5.5 or 5.6, and so on). This yields a directed edge:

$$\text{unlock: } (p_{\text{blocked}}, i_{\text{blocked}}) \ \longleftarrow \ (p_{\text{blocker}}, i_{\text{blocker}})$$

Aggregating these edges across respondents gives the **Unlock Value** of any candidate intervention:

$$U_{p,i} = |\{ r \in \text{Gated Supply}_{\text{any address}} : Cb\text{ tag of } r \text{ resolves to } (p,i) \}|$$

A high  $U$  identifies an intervention that returns multiple contributors to circulation. Rank interventions by  $U$  alongside raw demand: a blocker holding back eight gated contributors may outrank a need voiced by three people, because clearing it resolves both.

**Two failure modes this catches that demand ranking alone does not:**

1. **The invisible bottleneck.** A blocker with few or no direct  $Da$  declarations at its own address, but a high  $U$ . Nobody is asking for it because it is not experienced as a need in itself, only as the reason other things are impossible.
2. **The self-resolving cluster.** A sub-indicator where Gated Supply exceeds the Demand Pool, and the gates resolve to addresses where this same group holds free supply. These communities can unblock each other with no external resource at all, and only the edge structure reveals it.

**Receive-side chains.** Apply the same resolution to  $Db$  tags. A  $Db$  tag of *tried, was refused* or *eligibility/paperwork* points at an institutional failure rather than a resource shortfall, and should be read against  $\Delta$  from Step 5, where it will typically co-occur with the access-failure signature.

Cross-reference with Module 8: a high  $\mu$  combined with low network density scores confirms that the supply and demand exist but have no path between them.

*Constant: set  $\epsilon = 0.01$  throughout this document, in both  $\eta$  and  $\mu$ . It exists solely to prevent division by zero and is small enough not to distort ratios above the reporting threshold.*

### Step 7: Missing Data Handling

**Skipped items are not imputed to 3.** A rating of 3 encodes "Adequate Baseline", which is a substantive claim, not a neutral one. Non-response in welfare instruments is not random: respondents disproportionately skip items that are painful, shameful, or destabilising to answer, and those items correlate with low scores rather than median ones. Imputing 3 therefore systematically launders distress into adequacy and conceals precisely the respondents the instrument exists to find.

Handling rules:

1. **Compute all means over answered items only.** Divide by the count of responses received, not by 3 or by 7.
2. **Record the skip rate as a first-class variable.** For each sub-indicator, track  $\sigma_{p,i}$ , the proportion of respondents who left it blank, disaggregated by demographic slice.
3. **Treat elevated skip rate as a finding.** A sub-indicator with a high  $\sigma$  in a given group indicates a topic that is sensitive, stigmatised, or badly worded for that group. All three warrant human follow-up and all three are actionable.
4. **Flag high-skip respondents for outreach, not exclusion.** A respondent who leaves large portions blank should be routed to a person, not dropped from the dataset.
5. **Blank  $C$  and  $D$  fields are not skips.** Both vectors are optional by design and a blank simply means no flag was raised. They are excluded from  $\sigma$  and never imputed under any circumstance. A blank  $Da$  is treated as an absence of declared need, which is the safe default: it withholds action rather than assuming it.

Where a downstream calculation structurally requires a complete matrix, substitute the **observed median for that item within the respondent's demographic slice**, never a fixed constant, and mark the value as imputed so it can be excluded from any finding that drives an intervention.

### Step 8: Demographic Routing and Partial Administration

The full instrument is the complete map, not the expected respondent burden. Administration is expected to be partial:

1. **Universal core.** Administer Modules 1 (Recognition) and 7 (Sustenance) to every respondent. These planes are the most consistently load-bearing across circumstance and provide a common comparison spine.
2. **Priority-driven branch.** Use the Section 2c ranking to select the two or three additional modules the respondent has themselves nominated as most urgent.
3. **Demographic branch.** Route additional modules by respondent characteristics where relevant (tenure status, employment, caregiving responsibility, disability, age band).
4. **Module 8 and Section 2c are always administered.** They are short and they carry the connective and weighting information the rest of the instrument depends on.

Scores remain comparable across partially-administered respondents because every score is a normalised mean over answered items. A plane not administered is recorded as **not assessed**, which is a distinct state from a plane assessed and scored low. The two must never be merged.

### Step 9: Consent and Follow-Up Routing

The per-module consent checkboxes govern all downstream action. No respondent is contacted, referred, or entered into a match on the basis of their scores without the corresponding consent box being ticked.

Two consent states are collected per module:

* **Support consent:** open to follow-up about their own need in this area. Gates referral and outreach.
* **Contribution consent:** open to being contacted about helping others in this area. Gates entry into the Supply Pool of Step 6.

A respondent may give either, both, or neither. Absence of consent removes the respondent from action lists but **not** from aggregate statistics, which remain anonymous.

A high  $Da$  is a declaration of need, not a grant of permission. Both must be present before any individual is contacted: the  $Da$  flag establishes that help is wanted, the consent box establishes that contact is welcome. Neither substitutes for the other.

### Step 10: Open Response Handling

The seven module-level free-text fields are not decorative. The 49 sub-indicators are a fixed ontology and will not cover every need a community actually holds. Open responses should be reviewed for recurring content that has no existing sub-indicator address; such content is the primary input for the next revision of the instrument.
