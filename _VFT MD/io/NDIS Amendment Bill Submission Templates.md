## **How to use this document** 

## This document is designed to help you write a submission in your own words, with as little pressure as possible.

You do **not** need to be formal. You do **not** need to answer every section. Short answers are completely valid.

You can write:

* single words  
* dot points  
* sentences  
* or paragraphs

## **How to submit**

## You can upload your submission at [this link](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Senate/Community_Affairs/NDISFutureGenBill), as well as view other submissions for inspiration.

## **IMPORTANT NOTES:**

* ## **Make sure your submission is written in your own words\!** The committee will disregard submissions that use the same wording.

* Clearly say what you want the Government to do with the Bill (i.e. “I ask that the Government withdraw this Bill”)  
* Submissions made to Parliament inquiries **are protected**, meaning it cannot be used against you\! This being said, if you want to publish your submission, do it **after the Senate committee publishes it on the website**, and **only share the exact version they publish.**  
* You can ask for your submission to be published anonymously.

# **What changes are being proposed in this Bill?**

# (Plain-language summary)

This section summarises some of the key changes proposed in the NDIS Amendment Bill, in simple terms. It is not the full legal wording, but a plain-language overview of the parts most relevant to participants.

You can use this section to help you respond in your submission.

## **1\. Increased administrative and compliance requirements**

The Bill proposes changes that may increase administrative processes for participants.

This may include:

* More expectations to respond to requests from the NDIA  
* Tighter timeframes for communication or responses  
* More formal processes around managing plans and supports  
* Greater emphasis on compliance with administrative requirements

## **2\. “Not contactable” and participant engagement rules**

The Bill includes provisions that may affect participants who are not able to be contacted.

This could mean a participant’s supports may be paused, reviewed, or affected if they do not respond within required timeframes.

This may apply in situations where a person is:

* in hospital  
* experiencing a mental health crisis or burnout  
* unable to communicate due to disability  
* without support to manage communication  
* experiencing housing instability or other crisis

## **3\. Changes to reassessments and plan reviews**

The Bill proposes changes to how and when participant plans can be reviewed or reassessed.

This may include:

* More structured or restricted reassessment processes  
* Limits on when changes can be made  
* Reduced flexibility during crisis or changing circumstances

## **4\. Changes to how funding for supports can be adjusted**

The Bill includes provisions that may allow broader adjustments to categories of supports, rather than only individualised funding decisions.

This could mean:

* Funding levels for certain groups of supports may be adjusted at a system level  
* Changes may affect entire categories of supports, not just individual plans

## **5\. Increased use of administrative or automated systems**

The Bill may increase reliance on standardised or system-driven decision-making processes.

This may affect:

* How decisions about supports are made  
* How flexible the system is in responding to individual circumstances  
* The level of human review in decision-making processes

## **6\. Interaction with broader NDIS reforms**

The Bill sits alongside other reforms, including changes to how supports are structured (such as foundational supports and new system arrangements).

Some of these changes are still being developed, trialled, or implemented over time.

## **IMPORTANT NOTE**

This is **a simplified summary** of key proposed changes based on publicly discussed reforms and common provisions in the Bill.

You are encouraged to respond based on your own understanding and experience of disability and the NDIS system.
