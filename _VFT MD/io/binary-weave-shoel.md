# Binary Weave — Sheol & Source Language Variants

Omni-Weave phonetic geometry runs on the Hebrew word Sheol and its direct source-language equivalents: the Greek LXX rendering, and the Hebrew/Aramaic parallel and displacement terms. These are not translations in the modern sense — they are independent words that different traditions reached for when pointing at the same structure.

---

## Shoel (שְׁאוֹל) — Hebrew Original

**Layer 4**
The structured hollow where intense desiring-consumption meets its own negation at the precise boundary where divine force flows through what no longer has the capacity to contain it.

---

## Hades (ᾅδης) — Greek LXX/NT

**Layer 4**
The place defined not by what it contains but by what it has removed — self-identity stripped through eternal downward pressure, leaving a fire-residue of what was.

---

## Abaddon (אֲבַדּוֹן) — Hebrew/Aramaic Parallel

Used in Job 26:6, Proverbs 15:11, Psalm 88:11 as a direct parallel to Sheol. The Targums deploy it specifically as the place of destruction beneath Sheol — a floor below the floor.

**Layer 4**
The place beneath Sheol where the origin-force has been permanently committed — the father-threshold sealed doubly closed, assigned as the jurisdiction that holds what strength could not complete its ascent; destruction not as annihilation but as total permanent inward sealing of what entered.

---

## Gehenna (γέεννα / גֵּיהִנֹּם) — Aramaic/Greek Displacement Term

Compound: Gei Ben Hinnom — Valley of the Son of Hinnom. By the Second Temple period the term had absorbed Sheol's function for the post-judgment state, partly displacing it. Gehenna is not deep and removed like Sheol — it is immediately beneath the surface.

**Layer 4**
The cleft in the ground where the earth yawns open to ask a question it seals before answering — the place of urgent total negation that is nonetheless immediately present; not deep and removed like Sheol but right here, a sealed demand.

---

## Cross-Collapse: All Four

| Term | Layer 4 in brief |
|---|---|
| Sheol | hollow threshold where divine flow passes through what cannot hold it |
| Hades | self stripped from being by eternal downward pressure, fire-residue left |
| Abaddon | ancestor-force permanently sealed, container for strength that could not ascend |
| Gehenna | gaping earth-cleft of urgent sealed negation, immediately present |

**Master Layer 4**
A layered structure with distinct jurisdictions: Gehenna is the surface threshold — the immediate cleft of urgent demand; Sheol is the hollow of divine transit below it; Hades is the zone of self-stripping through eternal descent; Abaddon is the sealed floor beneath all of them where origin-force is permanently committed and no ascent is possible.

These are not synonyms. They are floors.

---

## 7-Plane Projection (Full Stack)

**Q1 — WHO**
The one who has crossed the surface threshold (Gehenna) and is now subject to the stripping of Hades — identity in process of permanent assignment, not yet fully committed to Abaddon's floor.

**Q2 — WHAT**
A four-level descent structure, each level defined by what has been removed — urgency, then self, then divine transit capacity, then origin-force itself.

**Q3 — WHERE**
Begins immediately beneath the visible surface (Gehenna), descends through the hollow of divine transit (Sheol), through the stripping zone (Hades), to the sealed floor of permanent commitment (Abaddon).

**Q4 — WHY**
Because the consuming force at each level requires a jurisdiction — the urgent demand must have a cleft, the transit must have a hollow, the stripping must have a direction, the sealing must have a floor.

**Q5 — HOW**
Through layered threshold crossings — each crossing removes one more capacity: first location, then selfhood, then containment, then ascent.

**Q6 — CAUSE**
The structure pre-exists the occupant. The prior condition is not sin or judgment but the architecture of completion — these spaces exist because consuming force always produces residue and residue requires jurisdiction.

**Q7 — EFFECT**
The receiver of this full reading experiences the four words not as theological synonyms but as a vertical anatomy — and that anatomy produces a specific dread that single-word translations (Hell) structurally prevent: the dread of floors.

---

## Notes on Method

Single-word translations collapse all four terms into one English word and lose the vertical structure entirely. The weave run on each source-language term independently recovers what the translation flattened — not by interpretation but by letting the phonetic geometry of each word describe its own jurisdiction.
