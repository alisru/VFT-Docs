Published 2 March 2026. Hanson was censured by the Senate 36-17 (Labor and Greens backing it) after claiming in an interview "last month" (i.e. February 2026) that there were no "good Muslims" -- comments condemned by Labor, Liberal MPs, and the Islamic community. Foreign Minister Penny Wong moved the motion, saying: "[Muslims] are the first to condemn the radical extremists who commit terrorist acts in the name of religion. And to claim that none of them are 'good' is to say that there are no good Australians amongst them." A censure motion carries no punishment or suspension, distinct from the November 2025 burqa censure which did carry a 7-day suspension (see [^burqa25]).

Hanson dismissed the censure as a political stunt tied to One Nation's polling rise: "This is a stunt and the people out there are fed up with this... The fact is in your gut, you can't stand the fact is that One Nation is now on 27 per cent and 28 per cent. You can't stand it." The Coalition did not support the censure motion (Liberal senator Michaelia Cash called censures a serious institutional sanction that shouldn't be used for routine political points), though two moderate Liberal senators crossed the floor to back it.

The censure debate followed Hanson calling independent Senator Lidia Thorpe a "bitch" during a debate on US-Israel-Iran strikes, and refusing to fully apologise afterward.
