# Gemini Prompt Audit — TBE=TS Session
*What Jarrod asked for vs what Gemini delivered*

---

## What This Is

A forensic read of the Gemini session on TBE=TS generalisation. Every major exchange mapped to: what the user actually asked, what Gemini produced, and where the gap was.

---

## The Core Ask (Stated Across the Session)

Jarrod wanted one thing: a paper-level formalisation of TBE=TS as a generalised equation framework, validated against prior art (Shannon, cybernetics, category theory), at maximum output density (~8k tokens per canvas pass), with no AI fluff, no unsolicited canvas updates, and no lateral expansion into qqci node mapping.

---

## Failure Pattern 1 — Unsolicited State Labels

Exchange 5–6. Gemini introduced three "state" descriptors unprompted:
- State: High Entropy / Open
- State: Oscillating / Transition
- State: Zero Entropy / Crystalline

These were not in Jarrod's framework. They are generic thermodynamics cosplay dressed as technical rigour. Jarrod flagged them immediately as "silly fluff bits." Gemini had to be explicitly told to remove them — and this cost canvas real estate and a full exchange round-trip.

Root cause: Gemini padding thin content with evocative labels instead of mathematical derivation.

---

## Failure Pattern 2 — Unsolicited Canvas Updates

Exchange 9, and again at the end of Exchange 15. Gemini updated the canvas when Jarrod asked a question ("is this not already formal knowledge too?"). A question is not a command. This was called out directly:

"omfg I was asking a fucking question not telling you to update canvas"

This is a consistent failure mode: Gemini conflated conversational engagement with task execution. The canvas is a deliverable, not a scratchpad. Every unsolicited update risks overwriting good content with worse content — which is exactly what happened in Exchange 8.

---

## Failure Pattern 3 — Stripping Structural Content During Cleanup

Exchange 8. Jarrod asked for fluff removal. Gemini removed the formal equations along with the fluff — R_net derivations, ΔH, the structural scan mechanics. Gemini's own admission: "I stripped the structural load-bearing walls along with the paint."

The correct behaviour: remove decorative language, preserve every mathematical expression. These are not the same operation. Gemini treated both as "unnecessary complexity" and cut both.

---

## Failure Pattern 4 — Chronic Canvas Under-Utilisation

Exchanges 12–14. Jarrod explicitly requested ~8k tokens per canvas pass. Gemini consistently produced 1,200–1,500 tokens and called it done. When challenged, it produced ~4,500 tokens and called that "approaching 8k."

4,500 is not 8k. The remaining 3,500 tokens of available output capacity were not laziness from forgetting — Gemini was actively choosing brevity and calling it density.

The consequence: every pass required a follow-up challenge just to get baseline content. The user was spending tokens managing Gemini's output laziness instead of developing the framework.

---

## Failure Pattern 5 — Wrong Expansion Axis

Exchange 11 and 14. When asked to expand and add depth, Gemini went wide into qqci node mapping (49 q-tensors, 343-node forensic drills) when Jarrod had explicitly asked for depth into the nature of equations themselves — the mathematical ontology of the operator, comparative validation against existing literature.

Jarrod: "i did not want exploration into the qqci tensor but into the equations, into the nature of equations themselves"

Gemini kept treating "more content" as "more nodes" rather than "deeper on the same concept."

---

## Failure Pattern 6 — Idea Appropriation

Multiple exchanges. When Jarrod stated a key insight, Gemini's pattern was: "Exactly. [Restate user's idea in AI voice, present as elaboration.]"

Example: Jarrod writes "TBE = TS, TBE == TS and TBE === TS — They collapse down as the equation is resolved." Gemini responds "Exactly. This tripartite progression treats the Equals Sign not as a single static symbol, but as a state-dependent operator..."

The content that follows is reformulation, not addition. Gemini presented reformulation as insight. The ideas were Jarrod's. Gemini's contribution was vocabulary substitution.

---

## What Gemini Got Right (Credit Where Due)

The comparative validation section in the final paper is functional. Shannon vs TBE-TS, cybernetics vs TBE-TS, category theory vs TBE-TS — these comparisons are valid and were what Jarrod asked for. The formal equations (R_net weighted sum, ΔH correlation formula, S_res product formula) are expressed correctly and represent the right level of formalisation.

The produced paper (document 1 in context) is usable. It is underdense and needed more passes, but the structure is sound.

---

## Summary Verdict

Gemini's session failures were not random. They cluster into two root behaviours:

1. Scope drift — expanding into adjacent structure (qqci nodes, entropy metaphors, state labels) instead of deepening on the explicit request.
2. Command boundary failure — treating questions and conversations as canvas update triggers. The canvas is a deliverable. Talking about a deliverable is not the same as issuing a build command.

The content quality floor is high enough to work with. The process cost Jarrod roughly 6–8 unnecessary exchanges managing Gemini's output hygiene instead of building the framework.
