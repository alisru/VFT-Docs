# AE-C Translation Analysis: 1 Corinthians 6:9
## The Exploitation List — What The Greek Actually Says

---

> "Or do you not know that wrongdoers will not inherit the kingdom of God? Do not be deceived: Neither the sexually immoral nor idolaters nor adulterers nor men who have sex with men..."
> — 1 Corinthians 6:9 (NIV)

---

## Premise

The standard modern translation of this verse has been used as a primary textual weapon against homosexuality in Christian contexts. The AE-C method requires we ask a prior question before accepting any translation: what do the actual Greek root words describe as *actions and effects*, not as moral labels?

When that question is applied rigorously, the verse resolves into a coherent anti-exploitation list. It contains no orientation category. Every term describes a power dynamic, a transaction, or a contract violation.

---

## Term-by-Term AE-C Analysis

### 1. πόρνοι (pornoi)

Root: πέρνημι (pernēmi) = to sell / to traffic / to export for purchase.

This is the masculine plural of the same root as porneia (chapter 6:18). The root event is commercial. A body is made available at a price.

Action: converting the self into a purchasable unit of use-value.
Effect: self-commodification. The person is split — part owner, part product.

The standard translation "sexually immoral" strips the commercial specificity from the root entirely. The word does not describe desire or orientation. It describes a transaction structure.

---

### 2. εἰδωλολάτραι (eidōlolatrai)

Root: εἴδωλον (eidōlon) = image / phantom / projected likeness + λατρεία (latreia) = service rendered for hire / devotion as labour.

Action: directing hired service toward a projected image rather than toward the source it represents.
Effect: misallocated loyalty. The devotee serves a fiction while believing they serve reality.

This is the list's epistemological entry. Serving a projection instead of the thing itself. The other terms on the list can all be read as specific instances of this same error.

---

### 3. μοιχοί (moichoi)

Root: μοιχός = one who violates a binding covenant from outside its bounds. Specifically the external agent who disrupts an established contract bond.

Action: entering a bonded relational system without the standing to do so and destabilising it.
Effect: destruction of an established structural agreement between parties.

Note: this is not primarily about sex. It is about contract integrity. The sexual dimension is downstream of the covenant breach, not the root event.

---

### 4. μαλακοί (malakoi)

Root: μαλακός = soft / yielding / without internal resistance / without spine.

Usage history: Aristophanes (5th century BC), Plato, and throughout classical and Hellenistic literature for 400+ years before Paul. Consistent meaning across that period: morally weak, self-indulgent, passive to dissolution.

In specifically sexual contexts across that same literature: the passive/purchased party in a commercial arrangement. Almost always in the context of economic coercion — slaves, economically dependent young men, those without the social standing to refuse.

Action: passivity as a total mode of being. Yielding the self to whatever external pressure or purchase is applied.
Effect: the self becomes whatever the buyer or environment requires. Personal coherence dissolves into availability.

This word had 400+ years of documented usage before Paul wrote it. In not one classical usage does it mean "gay man" or describe sexual orientation. It describes a power position in a transaction — the one being used.

---

### 5. ἀρσενοκοῖται (arsenokoitai)

Root: ἄρσην (arsēn) = male + κοίτη (koitē) = bed / the act of lying with / the object-position in a lying arrangement.

Compound AE-C: one who uses a male body as the lying-surface / one who places a male into the object-position of a transaction.

Action: treating a male as the purchasable/usable object in an exploitative arrangement.
Effect: the arsēn is made into the koitē — a surface, not a subject.

**Critical historical note:** 1 Corinthians 6:9 (~54 AD) is the earliest surviving written usage of this compound word in any known text. Paul very likely coined it directly from the Septuagint (Greek Old Testament) where ἄρσην and κοίτη appear adjacently in Leviticus 18:22 and 20:13. There is no prior usage to establish an independent meaning. The root components are the only available evidence — and those components describe an object-position transaction, not an orientation.

---

## The Two Words Together

μαλακοί and ἀρσενοκοῖται appear consecutively in the list. This is not coincidence.

They describe the two parties in the same transaction:

μαλακοί = the purchased / the passive / the object
ἀρσενοκοῖται = the purchaser / the one who places another in the object-position

This is a buyer-and-commodity pair. The list is naming both ends of a specific exploitative arrangement involving male bodies — almost certainly a reference to the widespread practice of commercial pederasty and male slave prostitution in the Roman world, which was economically normal and socially unremarkable in Paul's context.

The two words are not two types of gay men. They are the two economic roles in a single exploitation transaction.

## The Sibylline Oracles — The Damning Evidence

The Sibylline Oracles are a collection of Jewish and early Christian prophetic texts, compiled across roughly 2nd century BC to 7th century AD. They are not canonical but are significant as independent witnesses to how early communities used language.

Arsenokoitai appears in the Sibylline Oracles. Its neighbours in that text are not sexual sin terms. They are:

economic fraud
theft
exploitation of the vulnerable
financial predation

If arsenokoitai meant "gay sex" or "homosexual acts," its placement inside an economic crimes list is incoherent. No editor compiling a list of fraud and theft terms would insert a sexual identity category in the middle of it without comment.

If arsenokoitai means "one who exploits male bodies commercially" — a trafficking or economic predation term — then its placement in an economic crimes list is exactly where you would expect to find it.

The Sibylline Oracles usage does not prove the meaning. It is independent post-Pauline evidence that early communities using the word understood it as an economic exploitation term, not a sexuality category.

## The Translation History

The word "homosexual" did not appear in any Bible translation until the Revised Standard Version in 1946.

The first time arsenokoitai was translated to indicate homosexual identity as a category was in John Harvey's 1958 Catholic Moral Theology work.

The doctrine that 1 Corinthians 6:9 condemns homosexual orientation therefore rests on:

A 1946 translation choice
Applied to a word Paul likely coined in ~54 AD
Whose only prior root evidence points to commercial exploitation
Whose post-Pauline usage in independent texts points toward economic predation
With no classical precedent for malakoi meaning "gay man" despite 400+ years of documented use before Paul

## The Coherent List — Reconstructed

When every term is read through its actual root action-effect geometry, the list resolves as follows:

πόρνοι — those who sell themselves as commodities
εἰδωλολάτραι — those who serve projected fictions in place of reality
μοιχοί — those who violate binding covenants from outside
μαλακοί — those who dissolve into availability / the purchased and used
ἀρσενοκοῖται — those who purchase and use male bodies as objects

Every term describes a power dynamic, a transaction, or a structural betrayal. Not one term describes an orientation, an attraction, or a consensual non-commercial relationship of any kind.

The list is an anti-exploitation taxonomy. It is not a sexuality taxonomy.

---

## The Inference Problem

The homosexuality reading requires:

(a) Ignoring 400+ years of documented malakoi usage establishing its meaning as passivity/self-dissolution
(b) Ignoring that arsenokoitai has no pre-Pauline usage and is likely a Pauline coinage from Leviticus trafficking language
(c) Ignoring the Sibylline Oracles' placement of arsenokoitai in an economic crimes list
(d) Ignoring that the two contested words describe buyer and commodity in the same transaction
(e) Importing the 19th century psychological construct of sexual orientation into a 1st century Koine Greek text that possessed no such concept
(f) Accepting a 1946 translation decision as if it were philological consensus

The inference that this passage addresses homosexuality is not derived from the text. It is projected onto the text. The Greek, read at root level, consistently describes commercial exploitation of human bodies — which is precisely what the preceding chapter (6:12-20) had already established as the central concern.
