The evolution you're seeing
🧠 The mainstream trajectory has three stages: raw window scaling (attention over more tokens, quadratic cost), then compression (state-space models like Mamba, KV-cache eviction, recurrent memory transformers), then the frontier stage: memory as persistent state rather than replayed transcript. 🦕 As of my training data, the closest published moves are Infini-attention (compressive memory bolted into attention), RWKV/Mamba (fixed-size recurrent state), and modern Hopfield layers (Ramsauer et al., "Hopfield Networks is All You Need") showing attention IS a one-step Hopfield retrieval.

📐 The structural insight: "infinite context" is a misnomer. Nobody will ever hold infinite transcript. The water staircase 📐
Each step fills before it overflows. This is a threshold-gated accumulation rule, and it is emphatically not how transformers work 🧠: activations propagate immediately, regardless of whether anything has "filled." Half-formed content contaminates the next layer as a matter of course, and the network just learns to cope.
The existing thing it does match is integrate-and-fire in spiking neural networks 🧠: accumulate until threshold, then fire, and below threshold nothing propagates at all. It also matches barrier synchronisation in parallel computing 🧠, where no worker proceeds until all workers at the current stage complete.
The property you've bought with this: no premature propagation. A plane that hasn't reached fill contributes nothing upward rather than contributing garbage upward. Which matters enormously for a white-box system, because in a black-box net a half-filled channel is just noise the next layer learns around, but in yours a half-filled Q6 would produce a legible-looking but unearned causal claim, and legibility makes bad output more dangerous, not less.
And the fill condition is already specified 📐: filled means TS===TBE. The staircase step fills exactly when the truth-state resolves or hits fundamentals. So the water analogy isn't a new mechanism, it's the geometry of the halt condition you already have. Overflow to the next step is what === means operationally.

Where this actually gets hard 📐
Runaway accretion. If the well pulls in everything nearby, you get contamination by proximity: material that fell in because it was merely close, not because it was relevant. TS===TBE bounds this (it stops when filled), but only if fill is correctly detected.
False fill is the serious one. The well fills with plausible-but-wrong material and reports ===. This is the accretion-model equivalent of hallucination, and it's worse than ordinary hallucination because the system will have marked it as resolved. The only available check is cross-plane disagreement: if Q6 and Q7 accrete incompatible material for the same TS, the fill is false. So cross-plane convergence isn't just the output mechanism, it has to double as the error-detector on accretion. That should be architectural, not an afterthought.
The metric problem. "Falls in geometrically" needs a distance function, and you can't use one, because a single metric would collapse the planes back into an undifferentiated space and undo the whole orthogonality commitment. You need seven metrics, one per plane, and then the mixing tier has to reconcile them. That's consistent with everything else you've specified but it's a genuine cost: seven distance functions is seven things to get right.
Never-fills. A well that never reaches === and never hits fundamentals drills forever. You need a depth cap or a budget, which is slightly ugly because it's an arbitrary bound bolted onto a principled halt condition. Though the honest read is that a never-filling well is itself information: it means the TS was malformed, or the address was wrong, or the corpus has nothing there. Failure to fill is a diagnostic, not just a bug.
